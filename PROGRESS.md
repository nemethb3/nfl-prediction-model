## Master Plan Phase 2: Compression Investigation
- [x] Task 2.1: Diagnosed. Decomposed the known ~3.3x compression ratio into stages (phase3_diagnostic.py: diagnose_player_level_compression, diagnose_team_level_compression, test_rescale_win_projections). FINDINGS: (1) model-level compression is real and position-dependent - QB 2.74x, RB 4.47x, WR 4.81x, TE 2.76x (real prior-season std vs. predicted std) - an expected regression-to-the-mean property of any R2<1 model, not obviously fixable without accepting worse per-player accuracy. (2) Team-level AGGREGATION adds further compression on top of the model's own: QB player-level predicted std=0.0657 roughly HALVES to 0.0328 after the starter-pick + play-mix blend - so it's not just the model, aggregation compounds it. (3) MOST IMPORTANT, genuinely counterintuitive finding: the rescale test (multiply net_strength around its mean by factors up to the full measured 3.34x "correction", refeed through the ALREADY-FITTED win model) shows MAE getting MONOTONICALLY WORSE as the rescale factor increases (2.882 wins at 1.0x -> 3.481 wins at 3.34x) - NOT better. Reason: correlation is only +0.216-0.33 depending on stage, so projected values are dominated by idiosyncratic noise, not true signal; rescaling amplifies the noise right along with the signal, and since noise dominates, MAE gets worse, not better. CONCLUSION: compression and weak correlation are different problems - you cannot fix accuracy by simply widening the bands, because the underlying signal is too weak for that to pay off. Any real fix has to target the correlation itself, not just the spread. This directly rules out "rescale/widen bands" as a viable Task 2.2 candidate fix.
