## 2. DATA ACCURACY

### 2.1 🔴 CRITICAL — WR team assignments are stale for 30/107 real players (28%)

The user's own example (George Pickens) is real and confirmed: this
project's real per-week game logs (`player_weekly_stats.csv`, built from
real nflreadpy play-by-play) show Pickens playing for **DAL in all 18 real
2025 weeks** — consistent with the real 2025 trade — but
`fantasy_rankings_2025.json` shows him on **PIT** every week.

**Root cause, confirmed by direct inspection:** WR's `team` field is sourced
from `data/processed/wr_epa_projections_2025.csv`, a **preseason snapshot**
file, and is never refreshed against real per-week data. RB/QB/TE don't have
this problem — `_rb_projections()`/`_qb_or_te_projections()` both explicitly
re-derive `team` from the real per-week `recent_team` column, correctly
capturing in-season trades. WR's `_wr_static_fallback()` (formerly
`_wr_projections()`, renamed in the Phase 4 task) never got the same
treatment.

**Real scope, not just Pickens:** cross-checked all 107 WR players in the
preseason file against their real, most-common 2025 `recent_team` — **30
players (28%) have a mismatched team**, including real, well-known names
(DK Metcalf, Davante Adams, Cooper Kupp, Stefon Diggs, Christian Kirk,
Deebo Samuel Sr., Keenan Allen, Tyler Lockett, DeAndre Hopkins, Brandin
Cooks, and 20 others — full list generated during this audit, available on
request). This cascades: `opponent` (looked up by `team`) and
`opponent_defense_rank_vs_position` (looked up by `opponent`) are both
wrong for every game-week of every one of these 30 players, not just the
team label itself.

**Recommended fix:** in `_wr_static_fallback()`, replace the preseason
`team` column with a real per-week lookup the same way `_qb_or_te_
projections()` already does — merge in `player_weekly_stats.csv`'s real
`recent_team` by `(player_id, week)` instead of trusting the preseason
file's team column.

### 2.2 🔴 CRITICAL — 2 of 8 division leaders shown wrong in Weekly Summary (Section 5)

`generate_weekly_summary_dashboard_data.py`'s `division_leader_rows`
recomputes each division's leader via `div_rows.nlargest(1, "wins_actual")`
— a **simple, tie-unaware** "most wins" query — instead of reusing the
already-correct, tiebreak-aware `is_division_winner` field that
`generate_season_projections_dashboard_data.py`'s real `_compute_seeds()`
already computed (real wins **and** real point-differential tiebreak, per
the project's own documented seeding rule).

**Confirmed real impact:** at the real week-16 checkpoint, two divisions
have a real wins-tie at the top:
- **NFC South**: TB and CAR are tied at 7 real wins. The real, correct
  tiebreak winner is **TB** (`is_division_winner=True` in
  `season_projections_2025.json`) — but Weekly Summary shows **CAR** as
  the division leader.
- **NFC West**: SEA and LA are tied at 11 real wins. The real, correct
  tiebreak winner is **SEA** — but Weekly Summary shows **LA**.

Since Section 5 reuses one fixed week-16 snapshot for every week's "Season
Context" panel, **this wrong leader is shown on all 18 weeks**, not just
one. The other 6 divisions (no real tie) happen to show correctly, which is
why this wasn't caught visually — a tie-unaware bug only misfires exactly
when there's a real tie, which is also precisely when getting it right
matters most.

**Recommended fix:** replace the `nlargest(1, "wins_actual")` query with a
direct filter on `season_projections_df[season_projections_df["is_division_
winner"]]` per division — reusing the real, already-correct field instead
of recomputing a simpler, wrong version of the same thing.

## 6. WEAKPOINT IDENTIFICATION

### 6.1 By week

Real accuracy by week ranges from a low of **35.7% (week 5)** to a high of
84.6% (week 8) — week 5 is a real, single-week anomaly worth knowing about
but not actionable on its own (only 14 games, expected noise at that
sample size). More systematically: **early season (weeks 1–9) real
accuracy is 69.4%, late season (weeks 10–18) is 62.0%** — a real ~7-point
gap. Worth investigating in 2026 (possible causes: roster/injury
attrition compounding over the season, more playoff-implication-driven
unpredictable game scripts late in the year, or real regression toward the
mean as Vegas lines tighten around teams' true strength by then).

### 6.2 By game competitiveness

Real accuracy scales cleanly with spread magnitude: 56.7% on real
close games (spread ≤3), 64.5% on real medium spreads (3–7), 79.7% on real
larger spreads (7–14), 100% on real blowouts (>14, though n=5 is small).
This is the expected, sane pattern (closer games are harder to call) — a
confirming sanity check, not a weakness.

### 6.3 By team

CAR (25%, n=8) and DAL (28.6%, n=7) were the real hardest home teams to
predict this season; both are small samples (7-8 games) so this is
suggestive, not a strong signal on its own.

### 6.4 QB vs. TE — a real, still-open question

QB (44.7%) and WR (44.2%, post-Phase-4) are now the two weakest positions,
both using different real methods (QB: volume-only trailing formula; WR:
trailing actual-PPR average) yet landing in a similar range — while TE
(54.3%), using the *same* volume-only trailing approach as QB, is
meaningfully stronger. This suggests QB's real weakness isn't about
"static vs. dynamic" (that was WR's specific, now-fixed problem) but
something else — likely QB's real game-script dependency (a strong Vegas
favorite tends to run more/pass less late, suppressing volume in a way a
trailing-average can't see coming). Worth a real backtest in 2026, the same
disciplined way WR's fix was validated (leave-one-out, not just a plausible
story).

### 6.5 Data completeness

`opponent_defense_rank_vs_position`: 93.8% populated; `recent_form`: 92.3%
populated (both real, both absent only for week-1 rows by design, not
fabricated with a fallback — already documented project convention).
`injury_status`: 100% populated. Real injury-status data passed a sanity
check: 0 players marked "out" have a real recorded `actual_ppr` (correctly
excluded, since ruled-out players don't play), and 88.8% of "questionable"
players have a real recorded `actual_ppr` (played anyway) — both consistent
with how these tags work in reality, not a data quality problem.

### 6.6 Real outlier games (not errors — real fantasy variance)

The 10 largest real |actual − projected| misses (e.g. Kyle Pitts week 15:
projected 10.1, real actual 45.6; Jahmyr Gibbs week 12: projected 20.6,
real actual 55.4) are real, individually-verifiable boom/bust games, not
data errors — this is the real, inherent week-to-week variance in fantasy
football that no volume-trailing formula can predict, already disclosed
throughout the dashboard as a real limitation rather than presented as a
solvable bug.
